#### Literature anchors and collision notes

1. **AndroCT, Li, Fu, and Cai, MSR 2021.** The [Zenodo artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/6336104) describes 35,974 unique benign/malicious apps, each profiled for ten minutes on an emulator and a Samsung Galaxy S4, with method-call traces. Its README describes both environment collections and same-length/random-input coverage. This directly occupies app behavior differences across emulator and device and supports a granularity lane. CASE must not claim discovery of that phenomenon. The data-use terms require an institutional agreement, no redistribution, and no commercial use.

2. **Guerra-Manzanares et al., “Cross-device behavioral consistency: Benchmarking and implications for effective android malware detection,” 2022.** [Paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mlwa.2022.100357). The study executes apps on nine virtual/real collection devices, finds syscall/profile differences, and measures downstream detector degradation. This is a direct collision for broad cross-device instability and classifier-transfer claims. It does not establish CASE’s source-positive claim recurrence/report-scoping estimand.

3. **Dong et al., “Same App, Different Behaviors: Uncovering Device-specific Behaviors in Android Apps,” 2024.** [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09807). It studies device-specific behaviors in more than 20,000 apps using a static-analysis framework. It establishes that same-app device dependence is a known topic; its method and target are not source-only dynamic-claim recurrence scoring.

4. **SELENE Android Paper Artifacts / ARTEMIS.** The [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serrooT/selene-android-paper-artifacts) documents Android 10/API 29 and Android 14/API 34 environments, L0.5 and L1 evidence layers, run metadata, and finite Monkey-driven emulator observations. The [README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/serrooT/selene-android-paper-artifacts/blob/main/README.md) explicitly cautions that non-observation is not proof of inability, and states that SELENE focuses on compact semantic representation/provenance. CASE should use these layers as released observations and not imply ground truth.

5. **Risk-control methods.** [Learn Then Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052) provides general calibration procedures for explicit risk control; [Conformal Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02814) generalizes conformal coverage to bounded losses. These methods occupy formal risk-control methodology. Applying a method is not by itself a novelty contribution, and guarantees depend on the exchangeability/dependence setup. In CASE, calibration claims must state package/report grouping and domain-shift assumptions.

6. **DYNAMISM 2016–2023.** The [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/21280255) says the release contains same-app first/second execution subsets (1,049 benign, 772 malware in run 2), but the record is Restricted. It is a strong diagnostic opportunity, not audited empirical evidence here.

7. **KronoDroid.** The [maintainer repository](https://github.com/aleguma/kronodroid) and [dataset paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2021.102399) explicitly position the dataset around time and cross-device malware detection, so broad cross-device or temporal malware-classification claims are already occupied. Our actual CSV audit supports an external *descriptive* recurrence replication, not a novel classification claim.

8. **CIC-InvesAndMal2019.** The [official dataset page](https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/invesandmal2019.html) describes install/pre-restart/post-restart capture. A reboot-stage analysis is therefore not novel simply because it is added; CASE’s contribution would have to be a rigorously paired claim-recurrence and reporting-policy study.

9. **TraceDroid.** The [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/3665877) covers historical app static/dynamic profiles. It can motivate temporal behavior analysis but does not by itself supply paired target-context outcomes.

#### Search coverage and limits

The searches covered claim-level Android evidence portability, dynamic feature transfer, same-app context differences, emulator/device recurrence, SELENE/ARTEMIS, Android version behavior, evidence abstraction, report scoping, selective classification, LTT/CRC risk control, repeated-run Android datasets, DYNAMISM, AndroCT, KronoDroid, CIC reboot stages, and historical profile datasets. Primary artifact cards and papers were prioritized. Search absence is not proof that no close unpublished, non-indexed, or newly published work exists. Re-run a final citation/chaining search after a protocol freeze and before a novelty claim is submitted.

#### Defensible novelty statement

> We evaluate whether source-only, APK-level evidence properties improve estimates of recurrence for an individual recorded security claim in a named paired Android execution context beyond claim-specific persistence, and whether that signal can support a calibration-selected report-scoping policy.

This is a precise residual-novelty hypothesis, not a priority claim. It survives only if the frozen evaluation shows incremental proper-score value, valid package-level uncertainty, the broken-pair control fails as expected, and external evidence does not collapse to a dataset-specific artifact.

#### Required residual-novelty interpretation

The best remaining CASE distinction is the **decision unit and operation**: source-positive recorded claim × APK × named target context; source-only recurrence probability versus a fit-only indicator persistence prior; then a prospective report-scope decision. Evidence supports testing this as incremental value, not asserting it as unprecedented. SELENE’s measured Brier skill and broken-pair control support a real association, but a modest single-corpus signal is not broad external validation. A separate P11 POC now directly tests Android malware FL across two paired acquisition contexts, but local-only models beat FedAvg/FedProx and the tested CASE portability mask fails. This is bounded empirical mobile malware work, not evidence of improved collaboration in heterogeneous IoT networks, and it measures no privacy intervention. The hard PhD gate therefore remains unmet; CASE fit is limited to the mobile-app security/reporting chapter component.

Searches used phrase and citation chasing across Android FL, personalized FL, mobile malware concept drift, adaptive/reliability-aware aggregation, FDG, calibration/conformal and new IoT DFL data. Exact duplicate formulations are recorded in [Novelty Collision Matrix.md](Novelty%20Collision%20Matrix.md). This remains a targeted audit, not a systematic review; pre-submission citation chaining is still required.

This statement is distinct at the estimand and output level, while acknowledging that context-dependent Android behavior, cross-device differences, evidence abstraction, and selective prediction are established neighboring areas. ### Phase 3 addendum — harsh novelty audit for promising lanes (2026-09-23)

For each lane: closest 5-10 works, most direct competitor, what it solves, colliding statement, residual, why it matters, testability. Detailed link list: `Novelty Collision Matrix.md`.

**Lane P46/P26/P30/P52 — client-level operating points under app-market heterogeneity.** Closest: DATP (own, submitted), Sentinel (2025), per-sensor thresholds in federated IIoT anomaly detection, Bayesian-logit-calibrated personalised FL-IDS, FedHGCDroid, DW-FedAvg 2026, AndroIDS 2025, Droidware 2026. Most direct: DATP itself plus generic per-client threshold work. Colliding statement: "client-specific thresholds reduce FPR disparity under non-IID". Residual: the *mechanism* — on Android markets the local-threshold benefit is score-distribution shift, not label-prior shift (prior correction lowered BA .847→.802 while local tuning raised it to .864). Why it matters: it would say when DATP-style scope is needed on mobile. Dataset can test it (yes, done), but the result is a diagnostic, not a new method; residual is small and largely confirmatory of the thesis.

**Lane P27/P29/P31/P39/P40 — measured-heterogeneity collaboration policy.** Closest: FedCollab (2306.06508), How to Collaborate (2401.13236), adaptive collaboration (2507.06844), clustered FL survey (2501.17512), FedCPS, CO-PFL, APFL-style mixtures. Most direct: FedCollab (per-client coalition choice). Colliding statement: "choose per client whether to federate, cluster or stay local". Residual proposed: training-only heterogeneity/transferability signals predicting the choice for malware clients. **Tested and not supported**: gate ≈ always-FT; transfer graph −0.011…−0.024 BA vs blend; routing no better than global. Residual novelty is therefore not a positive method; at most a negative result ("heterogeneity statistics add little beyond client support n").

**Lane P44 — honest heterogeneity vs poisoned clients.** Closest: Heterogeneity-Oblivious Robust FL (2508.03579), FedCC, FLARE, FLAURA, adaptive trust aggregation, FedRoLA. Most direct: Heterogeneity-Oblivious Robust FL. Solves: benign-bias vs poison separation in generic FL. Residual: none of the returned results evaluates natural mobile-market clients; our POC shows Krum harms honest clients (−0.10 BA) and trimming ranks honest stores above a label-flip attacker. Untested: stronger attacks, malware-specific defence, false-rejection metric across many seeds. Substantive but unproven.

**Lane P38 — drift vs context.** Closest: FL-MalDrift (2025), M2FD (2025), FLAME (2410.01386), concept-drift Android papers. Colliding statement: "local drift detectors gate federated participation". Residual: a federation-referenced drift signal is dominated by stable store differences (cross-market JSD 5-7× one-year drift). Correction not built.

**Lanes rejected on novelty:** P36 (generic selective prediction), P43 (DATP-CP duplicate), P48 (APFL-style mixture), P45 (untested).

**Conclusion:** no lane keeps non-trivial residual *and* positive evidence *and* IoT relevance. Do not claim firstness for anything above.


#### Phase 5 G-NOVEL for P59 (contamination + verification budget), 2026-09-24

Five closest: (1) Bashari, Sesia, Romano, ICML 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04807) — conformal outlier detection with contaminated reference set; conservative type-I control; Label-Trim uses a limited labeling budget + detector to annotate suspected outliers in the reference set; single client; would collide with any claim of "labeled-budget trimming of contaminated calibration". (2) arXiv 2605.06204 — trimming as conditioning, retained-law diagnostic; theory, single-client; collides with "when trimming helps". (3) arXiv 2607.25439 — federated NIDS under anomalous-sample contamination in *training*, EM selective aggregation, unsupervised; collides with "contamination-robust federated IoT anomaly detection"; does not address thresholds. (4) arXiv 2410.09284 — federated AE with summary-statistics thresholding; collides with federated threshold estimation. **Most dangerous collision:** Bashari et al. Label-Trim.

Residual question: does sharing verified contamination counts across heterogeneous clients make a tiny budget sufficient? Measured yes vs per-client Label-Trim (worst-client gap recovered 32-37% vs 86-96%, k=10), but the effect is a shrinkage estimator and does not beat verified-benign+shrinkage consistently. **G-NOVEL verdict: WEAK residual (fail).**

#### Claim collision rules

- Do not say **first**, **novel**, **unprecedented**, or **unique** based on this targeted search.
- Keep claims attached to the processed observation unit and named data conditions. “Not observed” is not “cannot occur.”
- A new aggregator weight name is not novelty. It must add a distinct, independently measured signal and improve a decision outcome beyond direct competing methods.
- “Privacy preserving” requires a threat model and leakage analysis; decentralized storage alone is insufficient.
- The Android CASE residual is currently a different decision unit and report operation, not a new Android malware classifier, new FL optimizer, or discovery that context matters.
- A three-seed P11 context-client POC now rejects the tested CASE agreement-only feature mask and finds local-only models stronger than FedAvg/FedProx. Keep this adverse result in every account of the Android-FL lane.
